# Jacksonville Fall Market Update: The Market Isn't Stuck. Overpriced Homes Are.

If you've watched a few homes in your neighborhood sit for months, it's easy to think the Jacksonville market has stalled. We hear it from sellers all the time this fall.

The numbers tell a different story. Homes are still selling across Duval County. What isn't selling is the house priced for the market we had a few years ago. Here's what the latest NEFAR report shows, why some listings get stuck, and what we'd do if we were listing this fall.

## What's happening in Duval right now

NEFAR's September 2026 report is the most recent one available as of early November. It shows a market that has cooled a bit from summer but is far from frozen.

| Duval County | September 2026 | August 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Median sale price | $345,000 | $335,000 |
| Median days on market | 29 | 25 |
| Closed sales | 830 | 887 |
| Active listings | 3,139 | 3,057 |
| Months of supply | 3.8 | 3.4 |

A few things stand out:

- **Prices held up.** Duval's median rose 3.6% from August. One month can bounce around, but this isn't a market in free fall.
- **Homes are taking a little longer.** Median days on market went from 25 to 29, and closings dipped. That's a normal end-of-summer slowdown.
- **Buyers have more choices.** Months of supply rose to 3.8 from 3.4. More competition means your home gets compared to more listings.

Zoom out to the six-county region and the year-over-year picture is better than many people expect. Northeast Florida single-family homes sold in a median of **36 days** in September, compared with **44 days** in September 2025. The regional median price was $399,000, up from $387,694 a year earlier.

What about price cuts? Realtor.com data for the Jacksonville metro shows the number of listings with a price reduction in September was about 13% lower than a year earlier. Nationally, 20.8% of active listings had a price cut in September, the highest September share Realtor.com has recorded. So Jacksonville isn't seeing a wave of cuts. But the cuts that do happen tend to land on the same kind of listing: the one that started too high.

## Why overpriced homes sit

It comes down to affordability. NEFAR's Home Affordability Index for Duval fell to 82 in September, from 86 in August. Anything under 100 means a family earning the median income can't qualify for the median-priced home. Buyers are stretched, and every extra $10,000 shows up in their monthly payment.

Here's how that plays out for a listing priced above the market:

- **It lands in the wrong search.** Buyers shop by price range. A home priced $25,000 too high sits next to newer or bigger homes and loses that comparison, while the buyers who would love it at the right price never see it.
- **The appraisal catches up.** Most buyers finance. The lender's appraiser looks at recent comparable sales, not at what the seller hopes to get. Even if a buyer agrees to a high price, a low appraisal can pull the deal back to earth or end it.
- **Time becomes the story.** Days on market are visible on every listing site. After a month or two, buyers start wondering what's wrong with the house, and they offer accordingly.

## What to do if you're selling this fall

### Price for the first two weeks

In our experience, a new listing gets its best attention in the first two weeks, when buyers with saved searches see it for the first time. Price it right and you reach the most motivated buyers while it's fresh.

- Price from recent closed sales near you, not from your neighbor's list price.
- Look at what's active and pending too. That's your real competition.
- If a price change is needed, make it early and make it meaningful. Small, repeated cuts mostly signal that the seller is still chasing the market.

### Prepare like buyers have options, because they do

With 3.8 months of supply in Duval, buyers can walk away from a home that needs work and find another one. A little preparation goes a long way:

- **Fix the small stuff.** Dripping faucets, scuffed walls, sticky doors, burned-out bulbs. Buyers notice, and they add it up fast.
- **Clean, declutter and light it well.** Online photos are your first showing.
- **Gather your paperwork.** Roof and HVAC ages, permits, warranties and recent repairs help buyers and their insurance agents. Insurance is a big question here, and buyers will ask.
- **Consider a pre-listing inspection** on an older home, so surprises come up on your timeline, not in the middle of a negotiation.

### Use concessions strategically

Concessions are common right now. Redfin found that 44.7% of U.S. buyers received some kind of seller concession in August 2026, up from 42.6% a year earlier.

Help with closing costs or a mortgage rate buydown can sometimes do more for a buyer's monthly payment than a small price cut. That can keep your sale price, and the comps for your neighborhood, stronger. There are limits, though. For example, Fannie Mae caps seller contributions on a conventional loan for a primary home at 3% of the price when the buyer puts down less than 10%, 6% with 10% to just under 25% down, and 9% with 25% or more down. Other loan types have their own rules, so we always confirm with the buyer's lender.

## The bottom line

The Jacksonville market isn't stuck. It's choosier. Homes in Northeast Florida sold faster this September than last, and Duval prices held up. What buyers won't do is pay a premium for a home priced like it's still 2021.

If you're selling this fall, the plan is simple. Start at the right number, make the home easy to say yes to, and use concessions where they help a buyer more than a price cut would. The first two weeks will tell you a lot.
