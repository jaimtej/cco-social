# Orlando vs. Jacksonville: Which Is Better for Your Florida Move?

If you already live in Florida and you're weighing Orlando against Jacksonville, you're comparing two very different cities. Orlando is an inland tourism powerhouse in the middle of the state. Jacksonville is a sprawling coastal city on the St. Johns River.

Neither is "better" for everyone. Below we compare them side by side on price, insurance, beaches, jobs, commutes and lifestyle, using the most recent numbers we could verify. We live and work in Jacksonville, but we'll be fair to Orlando. It has real strengths.

## The quick comparison

| | Jacksonville | Orlando |
|---|---|---|
| Median sale price, Sept. 2026 | $345,000 (Duval, NEFAR) | $432,360 (single-family, ORRA) |
| Avg. homeowners premium with wind, March 2026 | $2,786 (Duval) | $3,610 (Orange) |
| Nearest beach | 22 miles of its own | About 1 hour drive |
| Avg. commute, 2020 to 2024 | About 24 min | About 26 min |
| City land area | About 747 sq. mi. | About 111 sq. mi. |

For reference, the Miami-Dade single-family median was $692,000 in September 2026 (Miami Realtors), and its average premium was $5,975 (Florida OIR).

## Home prices

Jacksonville is the more affordable of the two. The Northeast Florida Association of Realtors reported a **$345,000 median sale price in Duval County** in September 2026. The Orlando Regional Realtor Association reported a **$432,360 median for single-family homes** in the Orlando area the same month, roughly flat from a year earlier.

That's a gap of about $87,000. On a typical mortgage, it can mean a noticeably lower monthly payment, or more house for the same budget. Your lender can run the numbers for your situation.

Two notes. First, both markets have a wide range. Jacksonville's beaches and St. Johns County run well above the Duval median. See [Duval vs. St. Johns County](/blog/duval-vs-st-johns-county-where-to-live) for that comparison. Second, Duval homes sold in a median of 29 days in September with about a 3.8-month supply, per NEFAR. Buyers have room to negotiate.

## Insurance

This one surprises people. Being inland doesn't automatically mean cheaper insurance.

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation reported average homeowners premiums for policies with wind coverage, as of March 31, 2026:

- **Duval: $2,786**
- **Orange (Orlando): $3,610**
- **Miami-Dade: $5,975**

OIR notes that actual premiums vary by company, insured value, deductible and policy terms. Averages are a starting point, not a quote. Our [Jacksonville homeowners insurance guide](/blog/homeowners-insurance-jacksonville-2026) explains what moves the number, including roof age and wind mitigation.

Flood insurance is a separate policy in both cities. The City of Jacksonville points out that standard homeowners policies don't cover flood. Check the flood zone for any address you're serious about.

## Beaches and the outdoors

Jacksonville wins on beach access. Visit Jacksonville counts **22 miles of beaches**, and the city maintains over 400 parks and recreational spaces, per the City of Jacksonville.

Orlando is about an hour from the Atlantic coast. VISIT FLORIDA lists drive times of about 58 minutes to Daytona Beach, 1 hour 3 minutes to New Smyrna Beach and 1 hour 9 minutes to Cocoa Beach. That's an easy day trip, but it's a trip. Orlando makes up for it with the theme parks and attractions in its own backyard.

## Jobs and economy

Both cities have solid, diverse job bases.

**Orlando** is built on tourism, and it's huge. Visit Orlando reported a record **76.7 million visitors in 2025**, calling it the most visited destination in the U.S. Beyond hospitality, the Orlando Economic Partnership highlights the region's modeling, simulation and training industry, which is home to the Army and Navy simulation procurement commands.

**Jacksonville** leans on finance, logistics, health care and corporate operations. JAXUSA Partnership lists target industries including financial services and fintech, transportation and logistics, life sciences, and advanced manufacturing including space and defense. The region has a deep-water port, three interstates, and Mayo Clinic, one of only three Mayo campuses in the U.S.

If your job is in hospitality or simulation, Orlando may simply make more sense. If you work remotely, Jacksonville's lower costs go further.

## Traffic and commute

Both cities are car-dependent. The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 data puts the average commute at about **26 minutes in the city of Orlando** and about **24 minutes in the city of Jacksonville**.

The bigger difference is shape. The city of Jacksonville covers about 747 square miles of land, compared with about 111 for the city of Orlando. In Jacksonville, where you live relative to work and school matters a lot. In Orlando, the city itself is more compact, though the metro spreads across several counties.

## Lifestyle

**Orlando** feels like a central hub. The theme parks and attractions are right there, and the visitor traffic keeps the city busy year round. It's a strong fit if you love having entertainment close by and don't mind a drive to the coast.

**Jacksonville** feels more like a collection of towns. Beach communities, historic neighborhoods along the river and newer suburbs to the south. It suits people who want water, space and a slightly slower pace. The trade-off is the long summer heat, which both cities share, and a lot of driving.

## The bottom line

If home price, insurance and beach access top your list, Jacksonville has the edge. If your career is tied to tourism or simulation, or you want theme parks and a central location, Orlando earns its place. Either way, compare specific neighborhoods and get real insurance quotes, not just county averages.
