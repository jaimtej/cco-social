# Is Jacksonville a Good Place to Live? Honest Pros and Cons

It's one of the most common questions we get from people thinking about a move. The short answer: for a lot of families and professionals, yes. The longer answer depends on how you feel about heat, driving and doing a little homework on every address.

Below is our honest take. We'll walk through what Jacksonville does well, the real trade-offs, who tends to love it here and how to narrow down where to look. Every number comes from a source listed at the bottom.

## The good: more home for the money

**Living costs run below the national average.** The C2ER Cost of Living Index puts Jacksonville at 89.9, where the U.S. average is 100. That's about 10% below average, according to the March 2026 JAX Facts sheet from JAXUSA Partnership, the region's economic development group.

**No state income tax.** The Florida Department of Revenue puts it plainly: Florida does not impose a personal income tax. For someone moving from a high-tax state, that can change the math on a paycheck. Property taxes still apply, and they can change after you buy. We explain why in [why property taxes jump after buying in Jacksonville](/blog/why-property-taxes-jump-after-buying-jacksonville).

**Home prices are reasonable for a big Florida city.** The median sale price in Duval County was $345,000 in September 2026, according to NEFAR. Homes sold in a median of 29 days, and the county had about a 3.8-month supply of listings. That's a market where buyers have choices.

## The good: beaches, rivers and parks

Jacksonville has **22 miles of beaches**, per Visit Jacksonville. The St. Johns River runs right through the middle of town, so waterfront living isn't limited to the coast.

The City of Jacksonville says its park system maintains **over 400 public and recreational spaces**, and calls it one of the largest urban park systems in the nation. Whether you want a quiet trail, a boat ramp or a ballfield for the kids, there's likely one near you.

## The trade-offs

We'd rather you hear these from us now than discover them after closing.

### It's hot for a long stretch

Summer highs average about 90°F in June, 92°F in July and 91°F in August, based on NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals as summarized by Current Results. Winters are mild, with January highs averaging about 66°F. If you love sunshine, great. If you wilt in humidity, spend a July weekend here before you commit.

### Hurricanes and flooding are part of the deal

The official Atlantic hurricane season runs **June 1 to November 30**, according to NOAA's National Hurricane Center, with the peak around September 10. Even storms that don't hit directly can bring heavy wind and rain, so have a plan and know your evacuation zone.

Flooding is the bigger day-to-day question. The City of Jacksonville notes that Duval County has coastal, inland and river flooding. Two points from the city's flood page worth knowing:

- Standard homeowners policies **do not cover flood damage**.
- National Flood Insurance Program coverage has a **30-day waiting period** before it takes effect.

Flood risk can change from one street to the next. Check every address on FEMA's flood maps, ask the seller about past water, and get a flood quote before you're under contract.

### Insurance takes planning

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation reported an average homeowners premium (with wind coverage) of **$2,786 in Duval County** as of March 31, 2026. That's well below some South Florida counties, but it's still a real line in your budget. Roof age, wind mitigation features and the 4-point inspection all matter.

### It's big, so you'll drive

Jacksonville covers about 747 square miles of land, per the U.S. Census Bureau. Counting water, it's roughly 874 square miles, which makes it the largest city in the lower 48 states by total area, according to a Jax Today analysis of Census figures. (By land area alone, Jax Today found that a consolidated county government in Kansas edges it out, and several Alaska cities are bigger either way.)

What that means for you: most people drive almost everywhere. The average commute in the city is about 24 minutes, per Census data for 2020 to 2024. Living close to work, school and the places you go most will matter more here than in a compact city.

## Who loves it here

**Families.** More house for the money, yards, and a wide range of school options across the region. JAXUSA notes that Nassau, St. Johns and Clay counties rank among Florida's top 15 school districts.

**Outdoor people.** Beach in the morning, river in the afternoon, a park trail on the weekend.

**People relocating from higher-cost markets.** No state income tax and lower prices can stretch a budget a long way.

**Military families.** Between NAS Jacksonville, Naval Station Mayport and nearby Kings Bay, PCS moves here are common, and the VA loan makes buying an option for many.

**Who may struggle:** anyone who wants a dense, walkable, transit-first lifestyle, or who truly hates heat.

## How to choose an area

Because the city is so large, "Jacksonville" isn't one place. The beaches, Riverside and San Marco, the suburbs of Mandarin and the Southside, and the new-construction communities to the south all feel different.

The biggest early decision for many buyers is **Duval vs. St. Johns County**. St. Johns had a September median of $568,750, per NEFAR, compared with $345,000 in Duval. That gap buys you different things: schools, commute, lot size and, in some newer communities, CDD fees.

A few quick filters that help:

- **Map your commute** at rush hour, not on a Sunday.
- **Check flood zones and insurance quotes** for each finalist address.
- **Ask about CDD fees** in newer communities. Here's [what a CDD fee is](/blog/what-is-a-cdd-fee-jacksonville).
- **Visit in summer** if you can.

## The bottom line

Jacksonville is a good place to live for people who want space, value and the water, and who are willing to drive and plan for heat, storms and insurance. The trade-offs are real, but they're manageable when you go in with clear eyes. The key is choosing the right part of town for how you actually live.
