# New Construction Without an HOA in Jacksonville: Is It Possible?

"Can we get a brand-new home without an HOA?" We hear it a lot. Some buyers have lived under a strict association before. Others just want to park the boat in the driveway and paint the front door any color they like.

The short answer is yes, but it takes a different kind of search. Most new homes around Jacksonville are built inside planned communities, and nearly all of those come with an association. Here's why, where the exceptions are, and what you give up when you go without one.

## Why most new subdivisions have an HOA

Nationally, **65.7% of new single-family homes started in 2024 were in a community association**, according to NAHB's analysis of U.S. Census Bureau construction data. That's up from 64.8% in 2023, and close to the 67.1% peak in 2020.

The Census Bureau describes these associations as legal entities created to maintain a development's common areas and enforce its private deed restrictions. They're usually set up when the community is built, and membership is mandatory.

Think about what a new subdivision includes: entry signs, landscaped medians, maybe a pool or playground, and stormwater ponds. Florida has more than 76,000 stormwater ponds woven into its neighborhoods, according to UF/IFAS, and they exist to prevent flooding and filter runoff. Someone has to own and care for all of it after the builder leaves. In most new communities, that someone is the HOA.

## Where to find new construction without an HOA

### Infill lots in established neighborhoods

Jacksonville has plenty of older neighborhoods that were built long before associations became the norm. When a builder puts a new home on a single lot on one of those streets, the house generally joins the neighborhood as it is. There's no amenity center or pond to maintain, so there's usually no new association.

The catch: these homes are scattered and few at a time. You won't find them in a model home sales center. You find them by watching specific neighborhoods closely, which is where we can help.

### Building on your own lot

If you buy land and hire a builder, you pick the plan and the finishes. You also take on more decisions: surveys, permits, site work and a longer timeline. Financing land and construction works differently than a standard mortgage, so talk with a lender before you buy the lot.

### Resale in older neighborhoods

Not technically new, but often the most practical route. Many homes in Jacksonville's older neighborhoods have no HOA and no CDD. A well-renovated house can give you much of the "new" feeling with none of the dues.

## No HOA doesn't mean no rules

This is the part buyers sometimes miss.

- **Deed restrictions can still exist.** Recorded restrictions can stay attached to a property even with no association to enforce them, and they may be enforceable by later owners. Your title work will show what's recorded.
- **Historic districts have their own review.** If the lot sits in one of Jacksonville's local historic districts, such as Riverside Avondale or Springfield, the City says a Certificate of Appropriateness is required for exterior work, and new construction goes before the Jacksonville Historic Preservation Commission.
- **City zoning and code still apply** to fences, setbacks, additions and the like.

## The trade-offs, side by side

| | With an HOA | Without an HOA |
|---|---|---|
| Amenities | Pool, trails, clubhouse in some communities | Public parks and your own backyard |
| Neighbors' upkeep | Standards are enforced | Depends on neighbors and city code |
| Monthly cost | Association dues | No dues |
| Freedom | Rules on paint, fences, parking, boats | More flexibility, within deed restrictions and code |
| Shared repairs | Spread across owners | None to share, but none shared with you either |

Neither is "better." It depends on how you want to live.

## HOA vs. CDD: not the same thing

An HOA is a private association. You pay dues to it, and it enforces the community's rules.

A CDD (community development district) is a special district, not a private association, and you'll see it most often in larger new communities. Under Florida law, its assessments can be collected by the tax collector in the same manner and at the same time as county taxes. A home can have an HOA, a CDD, both or neither. We break CDDs down in detail in [What Is a CDD Fee in Jacksonville?](/blog/what-is-a-cdd-fee-jacksonville)

Florida law also requires that buyers in a community with a mandatory HOA receive a disclosure summary before signing the contract. It spells out that you'll be a member, pay assessments, and may owe special assessments to a county or special district. If you don't get it before signing, you can cancel within 3 days of receiving it or before closing, whichever comes first.

## If the home you love does have an HOA

That isn't a deal breaker. Florida's 2024 HOA law, HB 1203, took effect July 1, 2024 and added real protections for owners:

- Owners get at least 14 days' written notice and a hearing before a fine or suspension, and if the violation is cured before the hearing, no fine can be imposed.
- Associations can't fine you for setting garbage cans out within 24 hours of collection.
- Every HOA must adopt hurricane protection specifications and can't deny an application that meets them.
- Associations with 100 or more parcels had to post key documents on a website or app by January 1, 2025.
- New directors must complete education within 90 days, plus continuing education each year.

Before you sign, ask for the dues, the budget, the rules, and whether any special assessments are planned. Then ask the same about any CDD.

## The bottom line

A new home with no HOA in Jacksonville is possible, but it's the exception. Look at infill lots on established streets, consider building on your own land, or widen the search to older neighborhoods. "No HOA" can still mean deed restrictions or historic review. And if the right house does have an HOA or CDD, get both numbers before you commit.
