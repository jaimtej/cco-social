# What It Really Costs to Own a Home in Jacksonville

When buyers compare homes, they usually compare prices. That makes sense, but the price is just the start. The number that actually hits your bank account every month includes taxes, insurance, upkeep and sometimes community fees.

In Jacksonville, those extras can add up to a lot more than people expect. Two homes at the same price can have very different monthly costs. Here's what to budget for, with a simple example so you can see how it comes together.

## Property taxes: about 1.77% to 1.8% of taxable value

In Florida, property tax is set in mills. One mill is $1 for every $1,000 of taxable value. For 2025, the Duval County Property Appraiser lists these total rates:

| Area | 2025 total millage | As a percentage |
|---|---|---|
| City of Jacksonville (outside the Beaches and Baldwin) | 17.7412 | about 1.77% |
| Jacksonville Beach | 18.4452 | about 1.84% |
| Atlantic Beach | 17.2004 | about 1.72% |
| Neptune Beach | 17.8161 | about 1.78% |
| Baldwin | 18.9701 | about 1.90% |

The key word is "taxable." Your bill is based on the assessed value minus any exemptions, not the price you paid. Buyers are often surprised when the tax bill jumps in the year after they buy. We explain why in [why property taxes jump after buying in Jacksonville](/blog/why-property-taxes-jump-after-buying-jacksonville). For a specific home, ask the Duval County Property Appraiser.

## Homeowners insurance: about $2,800 a year on average

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation reported an average homeowners premium of **$2,786 a year in Duval County with wind coverage** (and $2,095 without wind), based on data as of March 31, 2026. That's an average across many kinds of homes. A newer roof, wind mitigation features and the home's construction can move your quote a lot in either direction.

We cover how to bring that number down, including wind mitigation and 4-point inspections, in our [Jacksonville homeowners insurance guide](/blog/homeowners-insurance-jacksonville-2026).

## Flood insurance: about $835 a year on average

Standard homeowners policies generally don't cover flooding. Flood insurance is a separate policy. Based on NFIP data, NerdWallet reports an average NFIP premium of **about $835 a year in Duval County**. That average covers all NFIP policies in the county, and the NFIP prices each property on its own risk, so your number could be well above or below it.

If the home is in a high-risk flood zone and you have a federally backed or regulated mortgage, your lender will require it. Even when it isn't required, it's worth getting a quote.

## Maintenance: 1% to 4% of the home's value a year

This one is easy to skip and painful to ignore. Fannie Mae suggests budgeting **1% to 4% of your home's value each year** for maintenance. Newer homes can usually get by closer to 1%. For homes over 30 years old, plan closer to 4%.

On a $350,000 home, that's $3,500 to $14,000 a year. In our climate, think roofs, HVAC systems, water heaters, pest control and exterior paint. Not every year is expensive, but the expensive years come.

## HOA and CDD fees: ask before you fall in love

Many newer communities around Jacksonville have an HOA, a CDD or both. HOA dues are usually billed by the association. A CDD shows up on your property tax bill, which is why buyers sometimes miss it. We break down how CDDs work in [what is a CDD fee in Jacksonville](/blog/what-is-a-cdd-fee-jacksonville).

Ask for both numbers early, and add them to your monthly budget, not just the mortgage.

## A worked example: the real monthly on a hypothetical home

**This is an example only.** The home, rate and fees are made up to show the math. Your numbers will be different.

**The assumptions:**
- Price: $350,000, with 20% down ($70,000), so a $280,000 loan
- 30-year fixed at an example rate of 7.00% (Freddie Mac's national average was 7.24% as of Oct. 29, 2026; your rate will depend on you and your lender)
- Taxable value of $300,000 after exemptions, in the City of Jacksonville (17.7412 mills)
- Average Duval homeowners and flood premiums from above
- Maintenance at 1% (a newer home)
- An HOA of $50 a month

**The principal and interest math.** The standard formula is M = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n minus 1).
- P = $280,000
- r = 7.00% ÷ 12 = 0.0058333 per month
- n = 360 payments
- (1 + r)^360 = about 8.1165
- M = $280,000 × 0.0058333 × 8.1165 ÷ 7.1165 = **$1,862.85**

| Monthly cost | How we got it | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Principal and interest | From the formula above | $1,863 |
| Property tax | $300,000 × 1.77412% ÷ 12 | $444 |
| Homeowners insurance | $2,786 ÷ 12 | $232 |
| Flood insurance | $835 ÷ 12 | $70 |
| Maintenance | 1% of $350,000 ÷ 12 | $292 |
| HOA (example) | Example fee | $50 |
| **Total** | | **about $2,951** |

So the "mortgage payment" is about $1,863, but the real monthly cost of owning this example home is closer to **$2,950**. That's roughly $1,090 more each month. Put less than 20% down and you'd likely add mortgage insurance too. A CDD would add more. Ask your lender to run your actual numbers.

## The good news

- **Insurance rates are easing.** Citizens Property Insurance announced an average 8.8% rate cut for its homeowners multiperil policies in 2026, taking effect July 1 for new policies and at renewal for existing ones. Other carriers set their own rates, so shop around.
- **Homestead helps.** If the home will be your primary residence, file for homestead with the Duval County Property Appraiser by March 1. You must own and live in the home on January 1 of the year you apply.

## The bottom line

The price tells you what a home costs to buy. The monthly tells you what it costs to own. In Jacksonville, taxes, insurance, flood coverage, maintenance and community fees can add a third or more on top of principal and interest. Get the real numbers on any home before you write an offer, not after.
